# planner: free design, every task (experiment two)

Designed by the model for every task on her map, with what it knows about her apps. Click paths from cards and recipes are checked by us. Every other step in an app names the feature it relies on and is not checked by us; its link is the vendor's own help page, found with web search, or none found. A click path from the model shows only beside such a link, and needs checking against it.

14 steps: 11 checked, 3 not checked by us (3 with a vendor help page, 0 with none found), 0 outside any app. 0 tasks left as hers.

## Answering messages

Level in the product: 4, Inside your apps, recipe gmail_help_me_write.

**Today.** You answer these many times a day, from scratch or by copying old emails. For new inquiries you check your Sheet first.

**The new way.** Gmail's Help me write drafts a reply inside the email you opened, and you add the details from your Sheet before sending. For quick texts with vendors, saved replies on your phone cover the same few answers. You stay the one who decides what each client or vendor hears.

**Setup**

1. Draft replies inside an open email from a short note about what you want to say. (Gmail). Relies on **Gmail Help me write**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: [Draft emails with Gemini in Gmail (Workspace Experiments) - Computer - Gmail Help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/13384326?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)
2. Save your most common short replies on your phone for texts and chats.. Checked: recipe phone_quick_replies.
3. Save instructions about your business and tone so drafts sound like you. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_about_you.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Reply starter notes: A short note per common situation, such as date check, budget question or call request, to feed Help me write.
* Quick reply list: Your most common short text replies, written in your voice.

**Where she checks.** You read every draft and check dates, names and prices against your Sheet before you press Send.

## Vendor booking emails

Level in the product: 3, Build once, reuse, recipe gmail_templates.

**Today.** You write these many times a day, often by copying an old email.

**The new way.** Your common booking emails become Gmail templates with clear gaps for vendor name, date and details. You fill the gaps from your Sheet and send. This replaces hunting for an old email to copy and the mistakes that come with it.

**Setup**

1. Make a project where you paste your best past booking emails so it can write clean template versions with gaps. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
2. Save the cleaned booking emails as Gmail templates you can insert in one go. (Gmail). Checked: recipe gmail_templates.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Booking email templates: Templates for first booking request, confirmation, deposit and details check, with marked gaps.
* Project instructions: A short brief telling ChatGPT how you write to vendors.

**Where she checks.** You fill every gap and read the email, checking the date and vendor details, before you send it yourself.

## Timeline planning

Level in the product: 2, Write it with AI.

**Today.** You build these by hand for each wedding.

**The new way.** You give ChatGPT the key facts for a wedding, such as ceremony time, venue, vendors and travel, and it drafts a day-of timeline in your usual structure. You adjust it with what only you know about the venue and the people. Starting from a full draft is faster than a blank page and keeps every wedding in the same format.

**Setup**

1. Make a timeline project holding your best past timelines as examples and your rules for buffers and order. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
2. Keep one timeline layout you can paste the draft into for each wedding. (Google Sheets). Relies on **Google Sheets**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: [How to use Google Sheets - Computer - Google Docs Editors Help](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/6000292?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)
3. For each wedding, paste in the key facts and ask for a draft timeline, then revise it by chatting. (ChatGPT). Relies on **ChatGPT project chat**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: [Projects in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10169521-projects-in-chatgpt)

**What Tally would write for her**

* Timeline project instructions: Your rules for pacing, buffers, vendor arrival and order of events.
* Wedding facts form: A short list of facts to paste in for each wedding.

**Where she checks.** You check every time, vendor arrival and travel gap against your confirmed details before sharing the timeline with anyone.

## Writing emails

Level in the product: 3, Build once, reuse, recipe gmail_templates.

**Today.** You write these many times a day.

**The new way.** Your recurring emails, such as inquiry replies, call requests and client updates, become Gmail templates. For one-off emails you speak your points and get finished text back. You spend your time on the parts that need your judgment.

**Setup**

1. Set up a saved assistant that writes your emails in your voice from a few bullet points. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.
2. Save your most repeated emails, like the call request to new inquiries, as templates. (Gmail). Checked: recipe gmail_templates.
3. Dictate longer emails out loud and get finished text back. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe say_it_out_loud.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Email templates: Inquiry reply, call request, client update and thank you emails with gaps to fill.
* Assistant instructions: Your tone, sign-off and what you never promise in writing.

**Where she checks.** You read each email and check names, dates and prices before you send it yourself.

## Chasing vendors

Level in the product: 4, Inside your apps, recipe gmail_claude_follow_ups.

**Today.** You write these by hand whenever a vendor goes quiet.

**The new way.** Each week you get a list of vendors who have not answered, then ChatGPT drafts a polite follow up for each one from your Gmail. Nothing is sent without you. You stop having to remember who is quiet.

**Setup**

1. Get a weekly list of emails you sent that have no answer yet, read only from your own Gmail. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe weekly_no_reply_list.
2. Have follow up drafts written for the vendors you choose to chase. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe gmail_claude_follow_ups.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Follow up wording: Your preferred tone for a first, second and final nudge to vendors.
* Weekly list request: The saved request that lists vendors with no reply.

**Where she checks.** You read each draft and approve it or send it yourself; nothing goes out on its own.
