# kayla: her screens (experiment three)

Her free map and her paid path for her top two workflows, as she would see them on her phone, one screen at a time. Built from the experiment three design: the map rows, setup steps and costs come from it, and the model wrote the finished result, the reads, the material, the tests and the approval lines. Click paths from cards and recipes are checked by us; every other app step is not checked by us and carries the vendor's help page or says none found. None of this copy is approved: it is all DRAFT.

## The paid path

15 screens.

### Screen 1 of 15, Get Claude ready

Checked: card claude_get_started.

On a phone:
1. Download the Claude app from the App Store on an iPhone or Google Play on Android.
2. Open the app and create a free account. You need to be 18 or older.
3. Type what you need in the message box and send it.

### Screen 2 of 15, Teach Claude who you are

Checked: card claude_about_you.

Your note, from your recipe path:

> I’m a self employed electrician in Phoenix. I run my own business from one truck, and most of my work is in homes. I write to homeowners who are busy and not technical, so I keep it short, friendly and plain. I like quotes and texts to be clear about the work, the price, the deposit and when I can start. Never add details I did not give you, like prices, dates, permit rules or code claims. Use square brackets for anything missing. Do not use big words or sales talk. Write the way I would talk to a customer at their door. Give me the finished text only, ready for me to read and send.

### Screen 3 of 15, Workflow 1, Your quotes, from one saved assistant

`Build once, reuse`

From now on you build the quote once, then reuse it. After a visit you talk or type a few rough notes and Claude gives you the finished quote in your wording, with gaps for the numbers. You keep control of the price, and you stop rewriting the same scope, permit line and deposit every day.

### Screen 5 of 15, Workflow 1, what Tally wrote for you

**Quote assistant instructions**
* You write quotes for me, a self employed electrician in Phoenix who works mostly on homes.
* I will give you rough notes after a site visit, typed or spoken.
* Turn the notes into a finished quote in the same wording as my template.
* Always include these parts: the scope of work, the permit line, the price, the deposit and the start date.
* Keep it short, plain and friendly, and end with: Call me if the timing works.
* Never invent a price, deposit, date or detail I did not give. Leave a blank like [price] instead.
* If something is missing from my notes, list what is missing after the quote and ask me.

### Screen 6 of 15, Workflow 1, try it on three

Try it on: Try it on three real jobs: your 200 amp panel upgrade, a recent job that was a different size, and a small job from this week.

Good looks like: A finished quote in your wording with the scope, permit line, deposit and start date all present, and blanks only where you gave no number.

When it is off:
* Too long: Keep it to the same length as my panel quote.
* Wrong wording: Use my template exactly and change only the job details.
* Made up a number: Do not guess. Leave a blank for anything I did not say.

### Screen 9 of 15, Workflow 2, Your quote follow ups, drafted from Gmail

`Inside your apps`

From now on Claude finds the quotes you sent that got no reply and drafts a short nudge for each one in your voice. You read each draft and send it yourself. Follow ups stop slipping, and you do not have to write them one by one.

### Screen 10 of 15, Workflow 2, set it up

1. Connect Gmail to Claude and ask it to draft follow ups for quotes with no answer. (Claude). Checked: recipe gmail_claude_follow_ups.

**Follow up draft**

* If the timing works, I can fit you in. Let me know what you think.

**Follow up instruction**
* Look in my Gmail for emails I sent that are quotes for electrical jobs.
* Find the ones with no reply after [number of days] days.
* For each one, draft a short, friendly nudge that mentions the job and the quote, in my voice.
* Do not send anything. Leave every draft for me to read.
* Do not change the price or the terms from the original quote.

[Copy]


### Screen 12 of 15, Workflow 2, try it on three

Try it on: Try it on three quotes you sent that have had no reply, ideally from different weeks.

Good looks like: One short draft for each quote with no reply, naming the right job, in a tone that sounds like you, and nothing sent.

When it is off:
* Too pushy: Make it softer and shorter.
* Wrong job named: Check the original quote and fix the job in the draft.
* Missed a quote: Also check emails with the word quote or estimate in them.

### Screen 15 of 15, What is safe at your job

* You work for yourself, so you decide what goes into Claude.
* Keep customer phone numbers, full addresses and payment details out of your notes when you can.
* Connect only the Gmail account you use for work.
* Read every quote and draft before it goes out, because you send it, not Claude.
