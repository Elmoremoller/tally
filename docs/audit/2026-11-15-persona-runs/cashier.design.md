# cashier: free design (experiment)

Designed by the model for her top two tasks with no fixed list of task kinds. Click paths come only from the checked cards and recipes, by id. Every other step in an app is its goal only and is not checked by us.

8 steps: 0 checked, 0 not checked by us, 8 outside any app.

## Handling returns

**Today.** You handle returns many times a day, each one from scratch at the counter. When there is no receipt, you need the customer's ID, manager approval and a price lookup, and it can take a while.

**The new way.** Because your company restricts AI, no AI tool touches your returns, your point of sale or anything from the store. The better way is a short return routine you write once in your own words and read before your shift. It puts the no receipt steps in the same order every time, so you are not working them out again at the counter. You still make every decision and get every approval the way your store requires.

**Setup**

1. Write down in your own words the order you follow for a return with no receipt: ask for ID, do the price lookup, get manager approval, then offer the options your store allows.
2. Add one calm sentence you can say when you have to decline a cash refund and offer an exchange or store credit instead, like the one that worked with the blender customer.
3. Check the routine against your store's own return policy or your manager, and fix anything that does not match.
4. Read the routine at the start of your shift so the order is fresh, keeping it out of any work system and free of customer details.

**What Tally would write for her**

* No receipt return routine: A short ordered list of what to ask for and check when a customer has no receipt, based on the ID, price lookup and manager approval you named.
* Decline and offer wording: One or two plain sentences for saying a cash refund is not possible and offering an exchange or store credit, for you to adjust to your own voice.
* Questions for your manager: A few points to confirm with your manager so the routine matches store policy, such as when approval is needed.

**Where she checks.** You check the routine against your store's return policy or your manager before you rely on it, and you follow the store's own approval steps on every return.

## Scanning customer items

**Today.** You scan customer items many times a day, and you named it as one of the two tasks that would save you the most time if it got easier.

**The new way.** Because your company restricts AI, no AI tool touches your scanning, the point of sale, the self checkout kiosk or the price lookup terminal. The better way is a short list of the scanning problems you meet most, each with the fix you already know works. You make the list once on your own time and keep it for yourself, so a stuck item or a missing price has a ready answer. Nothing connects to a work app, and your scanning stays exactly as your store runs it.

**Setup**

1. Over a few shifts, jot down the scanning problems that slow you down most, such as an item that will not scan or a price that looks wrong.
2. Next to each problem, write the fix you use now, such as the price lookup terminal or asking a coworker or manager.
3. Ask your manager whether each fix is the approved way to handle it, and change any that are not.
4. Keep the finished list for your own use and read it before busy shifts, free of customer details and kept out of any work system.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Scanning problem list: Your most common scanning problems in your own words, each paired with the fix you use.
* Manager check list: The fixes you want your manager to confirm as the approved way to handle each problem.

**Where she checks.** You confirm each fix with your manager and keep using your store's own systems and rules for every item you scan.
