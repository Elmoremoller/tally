# hr: free design (experiment)

Designed by the model for her top two tasks with no fixed list of task kinds. Click paths come only from the checked cards and recipes, by id. Every other step in an app is its goal only and is not checked by us.

12 steps: 7 checked, 0 not checked by us, 5 outside any app.

## Answering benefits questions

**Today.** You answer benefits questions many times a day, mostly by email and at your desk, and also by Teams chat. Your last one was a parental leave question, where you pointed to the benefits portal and said you would confirm the paid portion with payroll.

**The new way.** Because you are not sure your company allows AI for work, the first step is to ask and get a clear answer, and nothing here touches real work until you have it. If the answer is yes, you keep a saved assistant loaded only with your public benefits policy wording, and it drafts a reply from the question you paste in, with no employee names or personal details. You read each draft, check it against the portal, and send it yourself. Answers stay consistent, and anything the policy does not cover is marked for you to confirm with payroll.

**Setup**

1. Ask your manager or IT whether you may use AI for HR work, and what may be pasted into it, before doing anything else.
2. Pick the ten benefits questions you get most often and collect the approved policy wording that answers each one.
3. Get Claude ready on your computer, only if your company says AI is allowed. (Claude). Checked: card claude_get_started.
   * Go to claude.ai in your browser. You can also download Claude Desktop for Mac or Windows.
   * Create a free account. You need to be 18 or older.
   * Type what you need in the message box and click the submit button.
4. Make a project for benefits questions and add the approved policy wording as its reference material. (Claude). Checked: card claude_project.
   * Open the left side panel and click Projects, or go to claude.ai/projects.
   * Click New Project in the upper right corner.
   * Give the project a name and a description. Claude does not read these.
   * Click Set project instructions, write how Claude should handle this task, then click Save instructions.
   * In the project knowledge on the right, click the plus button and add your examples as documents or text.
   * Start a chat inside the project. Claude uses your instructions and examples in every chat there.
5. Set up a saved assistant that drafts benefits replies from the policy wording and flags anything it cannot answer. (Claude). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.
   * Open the left side panel and click Projects, or go to claude.ai/projects.
   * Click New Project in the upper right corner.
   * Give the project a name and a description. Claude does not read these.
   * Click Set project instructions, write how Claude should handle this task, then click Save instructions.
   * In the project knowledge on the right, click the plus button and add your examples as documents or text.
   * Start a chat inside the project. Claude uses your instructions and examples in every chat there.
   * Use the instructions and examples Tally wrote for you when the card asks for them.
   * Each time the task comes up, start a new chat in the project and say what you need.
6. Test it with three past questions that have names and personal details removed, and compare each draft with the portal.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Assistant instructions: Tells the assistant to answer only from your policy wording, to say when something needs payroll or you to confirm, and to never ask for personal details.
* Policy reference sheet: Holds your approved wording on leave, health cover and other common benefits topics, with the portal section for each.
* Tone notes: Describes how you like to sound with colleagues: warm, short and clear.
* Escalation list: Lists the question types that always go to payroll or to you, such as paid portions and individual cases.

**Where she checks.** You read every draft, check it against the benefits portal, and send it yourself, and nothing goes out without you.

## Writing emails

**Today.** You write a lot of emails, many of them repeats, covering onboarding, policy reminders and interview scheduling.

**The new way.** Your most repeated emails become saved templates in Outlook-style wording, each with clear gaps for names and dates. This works without connecting your work email to anything, since you only copy the finished wording across yourself. For the monthly newsletter and one-off emails, you say what you want out loud or in rough notes, and an AI tool turns it into a draft, but only if your company allows AI. You fill the gaps and read each email before it goes, so the repeats take less effort and still sound like you.

**Setup**

1. List your five most repeated emails, such as welcome note, interview invite, policy reminder and newsletter intro.
2. Ask your manager or IT whether AI may be used for drafting work emails, and keep real employee details out of any tool until you have the answer.
3. Get ChatGPT ready on your computer, only if your company allows AI. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_get_started.
   * Go to chatgpt.com in your browser.
   * Sign up with an email address you own, so your chats are saved.
   * Type what you need in the message box and send it. Select New chat when you want to start fresh.
4. Save short notes about your role and writing style so drafts sound like you. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_about_you.
   * Open Settings from the sidebar.
   * Select Personalization.
   * Make sure Enable customization is turned on.
   * Type your instructions in the Custom Instructions field. They apply to every chat right away.
5. Set up a saved assistant that writes your repeated emails as drafts with gaps left for names, dates and details. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.
   * Select New project in the sidebar.
   * Give the project a name, then pick an icon and a color.
   * Open the menu with three dots and select Project settings.
   * Type your instructions for this task, then select Save.
   * Add your examples to the project by uploading a file or pasting text.
   * Start a new chat inside the project. It uses your instructions and examples, and no other chat does.
   * Use the instructions and examples Tally wrote for you when the card asks for them.
   * Each time the task comes up, start a new chat in the project and say what you need.
6. Use rough spoken or typed notes to get a finished draft of the monthly newsletter or a one-off email. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe say_it_out_loud.
   * Paste the opening line Tally wrote for you into a new chat.
   * Press the microphone icon and say what you need.
   * Check the words ChatGPT heard and change anything that is wrong, then send it.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Email templates: Holds your five most repeated emails with clear gaps for names, dates and details.
* Assistant instructions: Tells the assistant to write in your voice, keep emails short, and leave gaps instead of guessing facts.
* Tone notes: Describes your usual greeting, sign off and level of formality.
* Newsletter outline: Lists the sections of your monthly newsletter so each issue starts from the same shape.

**Where she checks.** You fill every gap, check every name, date and policy detail, and send each email yourself.
