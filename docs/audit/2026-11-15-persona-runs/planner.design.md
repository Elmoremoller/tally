# planner: free design (experiment)

Designed by the model for her top two tasks with no fixed list of task kinds. Click paths come only from the checked cards and recipes, by id. Every other step in an app is its goal only and is not checked by us.

9 steps: 6 checked, 1 not checked by us, 2 outside any app.

## Answering inquiry messages

**Today.** You answer these many times a day, often by copying an old reply and checking the calendar first. You also look up the person's details before you answer.

**The new way.** Your most common replies are saved once, so a first answer to a new inquiry is a few letters away on your phone. You still check the calendar and look up their details, then fill the date and the person's name into the saved reply. Every reply is read by you before it goes out, and it keeps the same warm voice every time instead of depending on which old message you found.

**Setup**

1. Pick your ten most common inquiry replies, such as date open, date taken, and asking for guest count and budget.
2. Save instructions about you and your business so every reply sounds like you. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_about_you.
   * Open the sidebar and select your profile icon to open Settings.
   * Select Customize ChatGPT.
   * Make sure Enable customization is turned on.
   * Type your instructions in the Custom Instructions field. They apply to every chat right away.
3. Make a project for inquiry replies and put your ten replies and tone notes inside it. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
   * Select New project in the sidebar.
   * Give the project a name, then pick an icon and a color.
   * Open the menu with three dots and select Project settings.
   * Type your instructions for this task, then select Save.
   * Add your examples to the project by uploading a file or pasting text.
   * Start a new chat inside the project. It uses your instructions and examples, and no other chat does.
4. Save the ten replies so each one is a few letters away in text and chat. (her phone's keyboard). Checked: recipe phone_quick_replies.
   * Keep the replies and shortcuts Tally wrote for you open, above.
   * In any app where you can type, touch and hold the Emoji button or the Switch Keyboard key.
   * Tap Keyboard Settings, then tap Text Replacement.
   * Tap the Add button.
   * Type one reply in the Phrase field and its shortcut in the Shortcut field.
   * Do the same for each reply on your list.
5. Keep checking the date yourself before you pick which saved reply to use. (Google Calendar). **Not checked by us.**

**What Tally would write for her**

* Ten saved replies: Your most common inquiry answers, with gaps for name, date and details.
* Tone notes: A short description of how you greet people, how warm you are and how you sign off.
* Project instructions: Tells your assistant to draft a reply from an inquiry you paste in, leaving the date answer for you to confirm.

**Where she checks.** You read every reply and confirm the date against your calendar before you send it.

## Writing client emails

**Today.** You write these many times a day, usually from scratch or from an old email.

**The new way.** Your most common client emails are saved as Gmail templates, and for the ones that need thought you describe what you want to say out loud or in rough notes. Your assistant turns that into a finished email in your voice. You keep the personal parts and stop rebuilding the same email each time, so each one starts from something close to done.

**Setup**

1. List the five to eight client emails you write most, such as proposals, timeline updates and vendor introductions.
2. Save your most common emails as templates with clear gaps for names, dates and prices. (Gmail). Checked: recipe gmail_templates.
   * Keep the emails Tally wrote for you open, above.
   * In Gmail, click Settings at the top right, then See all settings.
   * Click Advanced at the top, click Enable next to Templates, then click Save Changes at the bottom.
   * Click Compose and paste one email.
   * At the bottom of the window, click More options, then Templates, then Save draft as template, then Save as new template.
   * To use one, click Compose, then More options, then Templates, and pick it under Insert template.
3. Set up a saved assistant that writes your client emails in your voice from rough notes. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.
   * Select New project in the sidebar.
   * Give the project a name, then pick an icon and a color.
   * Open the menu with three dots and select Project settings.
   * Type your instructions for this task, then select Save.
   * Add your examples to the project by uploading a file or pasting text.
   * Start a new chat inside the project. It uses your instructions and examples, and no other chat does.
   * Use the instructions and examples Tally wrote for you when the card asks for them.
   * Each time the task comes up, start a new chat in the project and say what you need.
4. Speak the points of a one-off email and get back finished text to edit. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe say_it_out_loud.
   * Paste the opening line Tally wrote for you into a new chat.
   * Press the microphone icon and say what you need.
   * Check the words ChatGPT heard and change anything that is wrong, then send it.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Email templates: Your most common client emails written once, with gaps to fill in.
* Assistant instructions: Explains your business, your voice and how your client emails are usually structured.
* Tone notes: Examples of phrases you use and ones you avoid in client emails.

**Where she checks.** You fill in every gap and check every name, date and price before you press Send yourself.
