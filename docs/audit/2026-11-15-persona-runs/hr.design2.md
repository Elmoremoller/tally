## Answering benefits questions

Level in the product: 2, Write it with AI.

**Today.** You answer these many times a day, mostly by email, but also at your desk and in Teams chat. Your last answer pointed to the benefits portal under Leave Policies and promised to confirm the paid portion with payroll.

**The new way.** You keep one saved assistant that holds your approved benefits answers and drafts a reply in your voice from the question you paste in. It points to the right portal page and flags anything it cannot confirm, such as pay details, so you check with payroll before you answer. You answer faster and more consistently, and the same answers work for email, Teams and walk ups. Since you are not sure what your company allows, ask first before you put any work content into an AI tool, and paste only the question with names and personal details removed.

**Setup**

1. Ask your manager or IT whether you may use an AI tool for work, and which one, before you paste anything in.
2. Get ChatGPT or Claude ready on your computer, whichever your company allows. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_get_started.
3. Make a project for benefits answers so your approved wording stays in one place for every question. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
4. Collect your approved benefits answers and the portal page names into one plain document with no employee details, and add it to the project. (Microsoft Word). Relies on **a feature it did not name**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: none found.
5. Set the assistant to draft short replies from your approved answers and to mark any pay, eligibility or date it cannot confirm. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Benefits answer bank: Your approved answers by topic, with the portal page to point to and who confirms what, such as payroll for paid portions.
* Assistant instructions: Tells the assistant to use only your answer bank, keep a warm plain tone, and flag anything it cannot confirm.
* Reply format: A short reply layout with the answer, the portal link or page name, and a line on what you are still confirming.

**Where she checks.** You read each reply and confirm every number, pay detail and eligibility rule with payroll or the portal before you send it.

## Onboarding new hires

Level in the product: 2, Write it with AI.

**Today.** You handle onboarding steps for each new hire, often with a checklist in Excel. The steps repeat for every person.

**The new way.** You keep one master checklist in Excel and one saved assistant that turns it into a welcome email, a first week plan and task reminders for each new hire from a few details you give it. You stop rebuilding the same messages and lists each time, and the checklist stays the single source. Ask first about your company's AI rules before you use any tool, and give it only the role and start date, never personal details like address or ID numbers.

**Setup**

1. Ask your manager or IT whether you may use an AI tool for onboarding material, and which one.
2. Tidy your onboarding checklist into clear columns for task, owner, due day and status, if Copilot is included in your Microsoft 365 and your company allows it. (Excel). Relies on **Excel Copilot**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: [Get started with Copilot in Excel](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/copilot/get-started-with-copilot-in-excel)
   * Where it is, from the model, to check against that page: Home, then Copilot
3. Make a project for onboarding and add your checklist with no names or personal details. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
4. Set the assistant to draft the welcome email, first week schedule and reminders to managers and IT from the role and start date. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.
5. Save the final welcome email text as a reusable block with gaps for name, date and manager. (Outlook). Relies on **Outlook Quick Parts**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: [Quick Parts](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/quick-parts)
   * Where it is, from the model, to check against that page: Insert, then Quick Parts

**What Tally would write for her**

* Master onboarding checklist: Every step with owner, timing and status, written so it works for any role.
* Welcome email draft: A friendly first email with gaps for name, start date, manager and where to go on day one.
* First week plan template: A day by day outline you adjust for each role.
* Assistant instructions: Tells the assistant to use only the role and start date, follow your checklist and leave gaps for anything it does not know.

**Where she checks.** You fill every gap, check dates and names against the HR system, and read each message before you send it yourself.

## Writing emails

Level in the product: 2, Write it with AI.

**Today.** You write a lot of emails, many of them repeats such as policy reminders and benefits replies.

**The new way.** You save your most common emails as reusable blocks in Outlook, and use a saved assistant for the ones that need a fresh version, like policy reminders or the monthly newsletter. The repeats take less effort and the wording stays consistent. Ask first what your company allows before pasting any work text into an AI tool, and leave out names and personal details.

**Setup**

1. Ask your manager or IT whether you may use an AI tool for drafting work emails.
2. List your five or six most repeated emails from your Sent folder so you know what to save.
3. Save each repeated email as a block with clear gaps for name, date and details. (Outlook). Relies on **Outlook Quick Parts**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: [Quick Parts](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/quick-parts)
   * Where it is, from the model, to check against that page: Insert, then Quick Parts
4. Make an assistant for policy reminders and the monthly newsletter that drafts from the key points you give it, in your tone. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Email blocks: Ready to use versions of your most common emails with gaps to fill.
* Policy reminder prompt: A short format for turning the policy name, date and action into a clear reminder.
* Newsletter outline: A monthly layout with sections you fill from your notes each month.
* Assistant instructions: Describes your tone, length and rules such as never inventing dates or policy details.

**Where she checks.** You fill every gap, check dates and policy details, and read each email before you send it yourself.
