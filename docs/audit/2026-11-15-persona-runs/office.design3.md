# office: her job as responsibilities (experiment three)

Designed by the model from her quiz answers. Her job is read as responsibilities, not writing tasks, each matched to the ten patterns and given the simplest workflow that removes the repeated work. Click paths from cards and recipes are checked by us. Every other step in an app names the feature it relies on and is not checked by us; its link is the vendor's own help page, found with web search, or none found. A click path from the model shows only beside such a link, and needs checking against it.

## Ordering lunch and drinks

Patterns: 4, Orders prepared from a short count; 6, Requests from colleagues, collected in one place; 1, Messages she repeats.

Level: 3, Build once, reuse. Held: you are not sure yet what your company allows.

**With her hands.** Collecting names and choices, then placing the order and setting out the food.

**Around it.** Gathering orders from email, paper, Excel and people in person, counting the dietary needs, and sending the order to the supplier each time.

**Today.** Orders arrive by email, on a paper sheet, in person and in an Excel list. You put them together by hand for about 70 people.

**The new way.** You use one Excel sheet with a column for name, choice and dietary need, and a count at the bottom that adds up each choice. Paper and in person orders get typed into the same sheet, so there is one place to look. The supplier order is written from the count with a saved template, so you stop rebuilding it each time.

**Built once.** an Excel lunch sheet with a drop down list, dietary columns and a count, plus a standard lunch order template with blanks

**On her map.** One Excel lunch sheet with a drop down list of choices, and a standard order that fills from the count.

**In her offer.** Your lunch order, from one Excel sheet

## Restocking the fridges

Patterns: 3, Stock that runs low and gets reordered; 4, Orders prepared from a short count.

Level: 3, Build once, reuse.

**With her hands.** Checking what is left in the fridges and putting new drinks and milk in.

**Around it.** Keeping track of what is low, deciding how much of each to get, ordering it and doing it again next time.

**Today.** You check the fridges and decide what is needed, such as oat milk and sparkling water. You then order it.

**The new way.** You use an Excel sheet listing each drink and item with the amount you want to keep and the amount left. A column shows what to order. You count the fridges, type the numbers and send the list, with no AI needed once it is built.

**Built once.** a restock tracker with the usual amounts and a column that shows what to order

**On her map.** A restock sheet in Excel with the usual amounts, so you count once and the order list writes itself.

**In her offer.** Your fridge restock, from one standard order

   * Vendor help page: [Overview of formulas in Excel](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/get-started/overview-of-formulas-in-excel)
3. Colour any item that is below the amount wanted. (Microsoft Excel). Relies on **Excel conditional formatting**. **Not checked by us.**

   * Vendor help page: [Quick Parts](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/quick-parts-4ffef7c5-7596-4e95-9faf-41c771847a7b)

**What Tally would write for her**

## Tracking office supplies

Patterns: 3, Stock that runs low and gets reordered; 6, Requests from colleagues, collected in one place.

Level: 3, Build once, reuse.

**With her hands.** Looking in the cupboards and replacing things like printer ink.

**Around it.** Knowing what is in stock, noticing what is running out, and ordering before it runs out.

**Today.** You track supplies yourself. When something is missing, people come to you and you find a replacement or order more.

**The new way.** You use one Excel sheet that lists each supply, how many you keep, how many are left and what to order. A second tab holds requests from colleagues, so missing items stop living in your head and in messages.

**On her map.** A supplies sheet in Excel with the usual amounts and a request list in one place.

**In her offer.** Your supplies tracker, with a request list

   * Vendor help page: [Use conditional formatting to highlight information in Excel](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/use-conditional-formatting-to-highlight-information-in-excel)
   * Where it is, from the model, to check against that page: Home, Conditional Formatting

**What Tally would write for her**
