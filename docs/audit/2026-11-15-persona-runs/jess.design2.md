# jess: free design, every task (experiment two)

Designed by the model for every task on her map, with what it knows about her apps. Click paths from cards and recipes are checked by us. Every other step in an app names the feature it relies on and is not checked by us; its link is the vendor's own help page, found with web search, or none found. A click path from the model shows only beside such a link, and needs checking against it.

11 steps: 11 checked, 0 not checked by us (0 with a vendor help page, 0 with none found), 0 outside any app. 1 tasks left as hers.

## Follow up emails to vendors and clients

Level in the product: 4, Inside your apps, recipe gmail_claude_follow_ups.

**Today.** You write these a few times a week, often from scratch. Vendor follow ups eat a lot of your afternoons.

**The new way.** A weekly list shows who has not answered you in Gmail, so you stop hunting through threads. Your chat tool then drafts a follow up for each person you pick, using the thread, and leaves it as a draft. You read it, fix the headcount or dietary deadline, and send it yourself.

**Setup**

1. Get Claude ready on your phone and computer so you can use it with your Gmail. (Claude). Checked: card claude_get_started.
2. Save a short note about your role, your tone and how you sign off, so drafts sound like you. (Claude). Checked: card claude_about_you.
3. Set up a weekly list of vendors and clients who have not answered you, read from Gmail only. (Claude). Checked: recipe weekly_no_reply_list.
4. Connect Gmail and have follow ups drafted for the people you choose from that list. (Claude). Checked: recipe gmail_claude_follow_ups.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Follow up instructions: Your tone, sign off and the facts every chase should include, like the event date, the headcount and the dietary list deadline.
* No reply list request: A weekly request that reads your Gmail and lists who has not answered, with the event and date each one relates to.
* Follow up draft prompt: A prompt that turns one thread into a short, polite chase that names exactly what you still need.

**Where she checks.** You read each draft, check every date, number and name, and approve or send it yourself.

## Answering messages

Level in the product: 4, Inside your apps, recipe gmail_help_me_write.

**Today.** You answer these throughout the week, one at a time. Many come through Gmail, with vendor texts on the side.

**The new way.** Gmail's built in Help me write drafts replies to emails that need a little thought, and you shape each one. For the short texts you send again and again, a few saved replies on your phone cover the usual answers. You spend your time on the replies that need judgment.

**Setup**

1. Draft replies to inbox emails with Google's own AI and edit them before sending. (Gmail). Checked: recipe gmail_help_me_write.
2. Save your most common short text replies, such as confirming a delivery time or asking for a headcount, so they are ready to insert. (Your phone keyboard). Checked: recipe phone_quick_replies.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Common reply list: Your five or six most repeated answers, written in your own words, ready to save on your phone.
* Help me write starters: Short instructions you can give Gmail, such as friendly, brief, confirm the date and ask for the dietary list.

**Where she checks.** You read every reply before you send it, and nothing is sent by itself.

## Building run of show

Level in the product: 3, Build once, reuse, recipe saved_assistant.

**Today.** You build these for each event by hand. Each one starts from the event details and your past events.

**The new way.** A saved assistant knows how you lay out a run of show: timings, vendor arrivals, service moments and who is responsible. You give it the event details and it returns a first version in your format. You adjust the timings and details you know from the room, so you start from a draft and not a blank page.

**Setup**

1. Get ChatGPT ready on your phone and computer, since you have used it before. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_get_started.
2. Make a project for run of shows where your format and a past example are kept. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
3. Set up the saved assistant so it returns a run of show in your layout from the event details you give it. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Run of show instructions: Your layout, the usual sections, how you write times and what to flag for the kitchen, bar and vendors.
* Example run of show: A template based on a past event with guest names and private details removed.
* Event details form: A short list of what to paste in each time: date, guest count, room, vendors, timings and special requests.

**Where she checks.** You read the run of show and check every time, name, number and vendor arrival before you share it with the team.

## Making lists

Level in the product: 3, Build once, reuse, recipe notes_to_sheet.

**Today.** You put these together for each event. The numbers come from Gmail threads, texts and Google Sheets.

**The new way.** You paste your messy notes, such as dietary replies, headcounts or vendor lists, and the assistant turns them into a clean table. You review the table and bring it into Google Sheets. Final numbers end up in one place, not scattered across threads and texts.

**Setup**

1. Get Claude ready on your phone and computer to turn notes into tables. (Claude). Checked: card claude_get_started.
2. Turn pasted notes into a clean table with set columns and bring it into your Google Sheet. (Google Sheets). Checked: recipe notes_to_sheet.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Table instructions: The columns you want for each list type, such as guest name, dietary need, headcount and vendor status.
* Notes paste prompt: A prompt that takes messy notes and returns a table ready for Sheets, marking anything unclear instead of guessing.

**Where she checks.** You compare the table with your original notes before you import it into your sheet.
