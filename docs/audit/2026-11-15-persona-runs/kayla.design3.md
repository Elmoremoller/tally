# kayla: her job as responsibilities (experiment three)

Designed by the model from her quiz answers. Her job is read as responsibilities, not writing tasks, each matched to the ten patterns and given the simplest workflow that removes the repeated work. Click paths from cards and recipes are checked by us. Every other step in an app names the feature it relies on and is not checked by us; its link is the vendor's own help page, found with web search, or none found. A click path from the model shows only beside such a link, and needs checking against it.

starter. Phone only. Can use AI: I work for myself. Where her work happens: Text messages, Google Calendar, Gmail, Google Maps, In person.

6 responsibilities, 0 left as hers, 6 with something AI builds once. 12 steps: 9 checked, 3 not checked by us (0 with a vendor help page, 3 with none found), 0 outside any app.

Patterns used: 1 (Messages she repeats) 5, 2 (Documents she repeats) 2, 7 (Routines and reminders on a schedule) 2, 8 (An inbox that needs sorting and follow up) 2, 9 (Notes that become tables or reports) 1.

Levels: Build once, reuse 5, Inside your apps 1.

## Her top two

1. **Quoting jobs.** You said it would save you the most time, and you write one about once a day.
2. **Following up quotes.** Unanswered quotes are lost work, and Claude can find them in your Gmail and draft the nudges for you.

## Quoting jobs

Patterns: 2, Documents she repeats; 1, Messages she repeats.

Level: 3, Build once, reuse.

**With her hands.** You look at the job at the home and note what the work involves.

**Around it.** You write up each quote from scratch with the scope, price, deposit and start date, about once a day.

**Today.** You write each quote yourself, like the 200 amp panel upgrade with attic runs and permit included. It comes up about once a day.

**The new way.** Claude is set up once as a saved assistant that knows how you word a quote: scope, permit line, deposit and start date. After a visit you say out loud or type a few rough notes and it gives you the finished quote with gaps for the price. You fill in the numbers and send it yourself.

**Built once.** a saved quote assistant with your usual wording, deposit and the blanks for price and start date

**On her map.** Your quotes, from one saved assistant that knows your usual wording and terms.

**In her offer.** Your quotes, from one saved assistant

**Setup**

1. Make a Claude project for quotes so your wording and terms stay in one place. (Claude). Checked: card claude_project.
2. Build the quote assistant from your 200 amp panel quote, with blanks for [price], [deposit] and [start date]. (Claude). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.
3. Try it by speaking the job notes after a visit and reading the quote it gives back. (Claude). Checked: recipe say_it_out_loud.
   * On a phone:
     1. Open the Claude app and start a new chat. Paste the opening line Tally wrote for you.
     2. Tap the microphone icon on the right side of the message box. The first time, choose your language.
     3. Say what you need, the way you would tell a colleague.
     4. Tap the arrow to send. Claude writes the finished text from what you said.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Quote assistant instructions: Tells Claude how you word a quote and which parts always appear.
* Quote template: Your panel upgrade quote with blanks for price, deposit and start date.
* Spoken notes prompt: A short line to start with when you talk through a job.

**Where she checks.** You read the quote and check the price, deposit, permit line and start date before you send it.

## Texting homeowners and answering messages

Patterns: 1, Messages she repeats.

Level: 3, Build once, reuse.

**Around it.** You answer the same questions about timing, arrival and what happens next by text, over and over.

**Today.** You answer homeowner texts and messages one at a time. Many of the questions repeat.

**The new way.** You save your most common replies as text shortcuts on your iPhone, such as on my way, running late and quote is coming. You type a short code and the full text appears. Then you add the details and send. There is no AI at all when you use it.

**Built once.** a set of saved replies for your most common homeowner texts

**On her map.** Your common replies, saved on your phone as text shortcuts.

**In her offer.** Your common texts, saved on your phone

**Setup**

1. Paste in a few of your own past replies with no customer names and ask for a short list of the replies you use most. (Claude). Relies on **a feature it did not name**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: none found.
2. Save each reply as a text replacement shortcut on your phone. (iPhone). Checked: recipe phone_quick_replies.
   * On a phone:
     1. Keep the replies and shortcuts Tally wrote for you open, above.
     2. In any app where you can type, touch and hold the Emoji button or the Switch Keyboard key.
     3. Tap Keyboard Settings, then tap Text Replacement.
     4. Tap the Add button.
     5. Type one reply in the Phrase field and its shortcut in the Shortcut field.
     6. Do the same for each reply on your list.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Saved replies list: Your usual answers on arrival time, delays, access and next steps, with gaps for details.
* Shortcut codes: A short code for each reply so it is quick to type.

**Where she checks.** You read each reply and fill in the details before you send it.

   * On a phone:
     1. Open the Claude app and start a new chat. Paste the opening line Tally wrote for you.
     2. Tap the microphone icon on the right side of the message box. The first time, choose your language.
     3. Say what you need, the way you would tell a colleague.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Invoice template: Your usual invoice wording with the deposit line and blanks for amounts.

**Where she checks.** You check every amount, name and date against the quote before you send the invoice.

## Following up quotes

Patterns: 8, An inbox that needs sorting and follow up; 1, Messages she repeats; 7, Routines and reminders on a schedule.

Level: 4, Inside your apps.

**Around it.** You remember who has not answered a quote and write a nudge to each one.

**Today.** You follow up quotes when you remember to. You write each message yourself.

**The new way.** You connect Gmail to Claude, which you control. Claude finds quotes you sent that have no reply and drafts a short nudge for each in your voice. You read each draft and send it yourself.

**Built once.** a follow up style for quotes that matches how you write

**On her map.** Your quote follow ups, drafted from your Gmail for you to approve.

**In her offer.** Your quote follow ups, drafted from Gmail

**Setup**

1. Connect Gmail to Claude and ask it to draft follow ups for quotes with no answer. (Claude). Checked: recipe gmail_claude_follow_ups.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Follow up draft: A short, friendly nudge that mentions the job and the quote.
* Follow up instruction: A saved line telling Claude which emails count as quotes and how long to wait.

**Where she checks.** You read each draft and press Send yourself.
