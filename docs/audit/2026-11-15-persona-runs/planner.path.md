### Step 1, workflow: Your inquiry replies, saved once on your phone

Your common replies are saved on your phone, each a few letters away. You type a short shortcut, tap the reply, and change only the name or the date. Inquiries come in many times a day, so the same answers stop being copied from old messages and are ready where you already type.

Recipe phone_quick_replies, route iphone + any_chat

On a phone:
1. Keep the replies and shortcuts Tally wrote for you open, above.
2. In any app where you can type, touch and hold the Emoji button or the Switch Keyboard key.
3. Tap Keyboard Settings, then tap Text Replacement.
4. Tap the Add button.
5. Type one reply in the Phrase field and its shortcut in the Shortcut field.
6. Do the same for each reply on your list.

Test: The next three messages that need one of these replies. Good: The reply fits after you change at most a name or a time.
Approval point: You read every reply before you send it. Nothing is sent by itself.
Cost: Text replacement on an iPhone, text shortcuts on a Samsung phone and the Gboard clipboard are free, and Tally writes the replies for you.

**Your inquiry replies**
* Hi [first name]! Thanks so much for your inquiry. [Date] is still open. Could you send me your rough guest count and budget range so I can put together options for you?
* Hi [first name]! Thank you for reaching out and congratulations. I would love to hear more about your day. What season and rough guest count are you picturing?
* Hi [first name]! Thanks for your message. I’m checking my calendar for [date] and will get back to you by [day] with an answer.
* Hi [first name]! Unfortunately [date] is already booked. I do have [other date] open if that could work for you. Want me to send details?
* Hi [first name]! I’d love to talk it through. Are you free for a quick call on [day and time]? If not, send me a few times that suit you.
* Hi [first name]! Just checking in on my last message. Do you still want to go ahead with [next step]? No pressure, I’m happy to answer any questions.
* Hi [first name]! Thanks for sending that over. I’m putting together options for you now and will send them by [day].

### Step 2, workflow: Your client emails, saved once as Gmail templates

Your common emails are saved in Gmail as templates, ready to fill in. You pick the template, fill the gaps, read it through, and send. Client emails come up many times a day, so each one starts from your own wording instead of an old email you have to clean up.

Recipe gmail_templates, route gmail + any_chat

On a computer:
1. Keep the emails Tally wrote for you open, above.
2. In Gmail, click Settings at the top right, then See all settings.
3. Click Advanced at the top, click Enable next to Templates, then click Save Changes at the bottom.
4. Click Compose and paste one email.
5. At the bottom of the window, click More options, then Templates, then Save draft as template, then Save as new template.
6. To use one, click Compose, then More options, then Templates, and pick it under Insert template.

Test: The next three times you would write one of these emails. Good: You fill in the gaps and send it without rewriting anything else.
Approval point: You fill in every gap and read the email before you send it yourself.
Cost: Gmail templates are free, and Tally writes the emails for you.

### Step 3, end

* Never type a couple’s full names, phone numbers, home addresses or email addresses into ChatGPT. Use brackets instead.
* Keep contracts, payment details and card or bank information out of it.
* Leave out guest lists and any private family or health details a client shared with you.
* Read every reply and email before it goes out, and check dates, prices and vendor details against your own calendar and contracts.
* Fill in names, dates and venues yourself, after the draft is written.
