## Post captions

Level in the product: 3, Build once, reuse, recipe saved_assistant.

**Today.** You write a caption about once a day, each one from scratch. You start from the bakers' photos and last week's posts.

**The new way.** You set up one saved assistant that knows your bakery voice and your caption habits. Each day you give it the photo details and a few words, like flaky layers and come in before 10, and it gives you caption options. You pick one and fix it, so the voice stays the same and the blank page goes away.

**Setup**

1. Ask your company whether you may use AI tools for work, and wait for the answer before pasting anything work related.
2. Get ChatGPT ready on your phone and computer. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_get_started.
3. Make a project called Captions to hold your voice notes and examples. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
4. Save instructions and three or four of your best past captions, including the croissant one, so it writes in your voice. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Caption instructions: Your bakery voice, caption length, emoji habits, and the usual closing lines like opening hours or come in early.
* Example captions: A few of your best past captions, so the assistant copies your style.
* Daily prompt: A short fill in message for the product name, what the photo shows, and any offer or time limit.

**Where she checks.** You read every caption and check product names, times and offers before you post.

## Comment replies

Level in the product: 3, Build once, reuse, recipe phone_quick_replies.

**Today.** You reply to comments one by one as they come in.

**The new way.** You save your most common replies, like thanks, opening hours and where to find a product, on your phone. When a comment arrives you insert the closest one and add a personal touch. Replies stay quick and warm, and personal or tricky comments still get your own words.

**Setup**

1. Ask your company whether you may use AI tools for work before drafting replies with AI.
2. Save your most common comment replies as text shortcuts on your phone keyboard.. Checked: recipe phone_quick_replies.
3. Use your Captions project to turn a list of your usual replies into short, friendly versions in your voice, without pasting customers' names or private details. (ChatGPT). Relies on **ChatGPT project**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: [Projects in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10169521-projects-in-chatgpt)

**What Tally would write for her**

* Reply list: Ten or so common comment replies, such as thanks, hours, locations, allergens and preorders.
* Shortcut names: Short keyboard shortcuts for each reply so you can find them fast.

**Where she checks.** You read each reply and adjust it to the comment before you send it.

## Weekly content planning

Level in the product: 2, Write it with AI.

**Today.** You plan the week's posts by hand.

**The new way.** You give the assistant your last week's posts and what is coming up, like new bakes, seasonal items and events. It proposes a weekly plan with a post idea for each day and a mix of topics. You change it to fit what the bakers are really making, so planning starts from a draft instead of a blank page.

**Setup**

1. Ask your company whether you may use AI tools for work before pasting your posts or plans.
2. Make a project called Weekly plan for your content planning chats. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
3. Save instructions about your role, your bakery chain and your channels so every plan starts with that context. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_about_you.
4. Keep a simple content calendar with a row per day so the plan can be pasted in and tracked. (Google Sheets). Relies on **Google Sheets**. **Not checked by us.**
   * Vendor help page: [How to use Google Sheets - Computer - Google Docs Editors Help](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/6000292?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)

**What Tally would write for her**

* Planning prompt: A message that takes last week's posts and this week's bakes and returns a daily plan.
* Content mix guide: The kinds of posts you want each week, such as new bakes, behind the scenes and offers.
* Calendar layout: Columns for day, channel, idea, photo needed and status.

**Where she checks.** You review the plan against what the bakers will make and approve each post before it is scheduled.

## Emails

Level in the product: 3, Build once, reuse, recipe gmail_templates.

**Today.** You write emails by hand each time.

**The new way.** You save your most common emails as Gmail templates with clear gaps for names, dates and details. When one is needed you open the template, fill the gaps and send it yourself. Your emails stay consistent and you stop retyping the same text.

**Setup**

1. Ask your company whether you may use AI tools for work and whether you may link work email to one. Do not connect Gmail until they say yes.
2. Turn your most common emails into Gmail templates with gaps to fill in. (Gmail). Checked: recipe gmail_templates.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Email templates: Ready to fill drafts for your most frequent emails, with marked gaps for names, dates and details.
* Gap checklist: A short list of what to fill in for each template.

**Where she checks.** You fill in every gap and read the email before you send it yourself.
