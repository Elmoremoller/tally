# office: free design (experiment)

Designed by the model for her top two tasks with no fixed list of task kinds. Click paths come only from the checked cards and recipes, by id. Every other step in an app is its goal only and is not checked by us.

11 steps: 8 checked, 0 not checked by us, 3 outside any app.

## Supplier emails

**Today.** You write supplier emails often, and each one takes a fresh start. Your work happens in Outlook, text messages and on paper.

**The new way.** You keep one saved assistant that knows your usual suppliers, your tone and the details a supplier needs, like item, amount and delivery day. You type or paste the few facts for each order, such as oat milk and sparkling water for the fridges, and it hands back a finished draft. You copy it into Outlook yourself, so the AI never touches your work email. Each email stops being a fresh start, and nothing about your company's rules is put at risk while you wait to hear whether AI is allowed.

**Setup**

1. Ask your company whether you may use an AI tool for work, and keep every step below on hold until you have an answer.
2. Get Claude ready on your phone and computer. (Claude). Checked: card claude_get_started.
   * Download the Claude app from the App Store on an iPhone or Google Play on Android.
   * Open the app and create a free account. You need to be 18 or older.
   * Type what you need in the message box and send it.
3. Make a project for supplier emails where your saved instructions and examples live. (Claude). Checked: card claude_project.
   * Open the left side panel and click Projects, or go to claude.ai/projects.
   * Click New Project in the upper right corner.
   * Give the project a name and a description. Claude does not read these.
   * Click Set project instructions, write how Claude should handle this task, then click Save instructions.
   * In the project knowledge on the right, click the plus button and add your examples as documents or text.
   * Start a chat inside the project. Claude uses your instructions and examples in every chat there.
4. Choose two or three past supplier emails you were happy with, with private details removed, to show your style.
5. Save the assistant so a few facts typed in give you a supplier email draft in your tone. (Claude). Checked: recipe saved_assistant.
   * Open the left side panel and click Projects, or go to claude.ai/projects.
   * Click New Project in the upper right corner.
   * Give the project a name and a description. Claude does not read these.
   * Click Set project instructions, write how Claude should handle this task, then click Save instructions.
   * In the project knowledge on the right, click the plus button and add your examples as documents or text.
   * Start a chat inside the project. Claude uses your instructions and examples in every chat there.
   * Use the instructions and examples Tally wrote for you when the card asks for them.
   * Each time the task comes up, start a new chat in the project and say what you need.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Assistant instructions: Tells the assistant you are an office coordinator for about 70 people and that it writes short, polite supplier emails from the facts you give.
* Tone notes: Describes how you like to sound with suppliers, such as friendly, direct and clear about dates.
* Supplier fact sheet: Lists your usual suppliers, what you order from each and the details every order email must include, with no private personal data.
* Example emails: Holds your best past supplier emails with private details removed, so the drafts match your style.

**Where she checks.** You read each draft and check every item, amount, date and name before you copy it into Outlook and send it yourself.

## Planning company events

**Today.** You plan events and write the details out by hand. Your work happens in Excel, Outlook, on paper and in person.

**The new way.** You say out loud what the event needs, in your own words, and the AI turns it into a clean plan with a checklist, a supplier to do list and a short announcement for the team. You keep the plan in Excel as you do now, and you paste in text rather than connecting any work app. Planning gets more organized, and you stop rewriting the same details in different places.

**Setup**

1. Ask your company whether AI may be used for work, and wait for the answer before using it on any event details.
2. Get ChatGPT ready on your phone and computer. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_get_started.
   * Open the App Store on an iPhone or Google Play on Android and search for OpenAI ChatGPT.
   * Check that the app comes from OpenAI before you download it.
   * Open the app and sign up with an email address you own, so your chats are saved.
   * Type what you need in the message box and send it.
3. Save a short note about your role so every answer fits an office coordinator for about 70 people. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_about_you.
   * Open the sidebar and select your profile icon to open Settings.
   * Select Customize ChatGPT.
   * Make sure Enable customization is turned on.
   * Type your instructions in the Custom Instructions field. They apply to every chat right away.
4. Make a project for company events that holds your event instructions and past plans. (ChatGPT). Checked: card chatgpt_project.
   * Select New project in the sidebar.
   * Give the project a name, then pick an icon and a color.
   * Open the menu with three dots and select Project settings.
   * Type your instructions for this task, then select Save.
   * Add your examples to the project by uploading a file or pasting text.
   * Start a new chat inside the project. It uses your instructions and examples, and no other chat does.
5. Speak your event details in your own words and get back a finished plan and announcement text. (ChatGPT). Checked: recipe say_it_out_loud.
   * Paste the opening line Tally wrote for you into a new chat.
   * Press the microphone icon and say what you need.
   * Check the words ChatGPT heard and change anything that is wrong, then send it.
6. Turn your messy event notes into a clean table you can paste into your Excel list. (Excel). Checked: recipe notes_to_sheet.
   * In Claude, open Settings, then Capabilities, and turn on Code execution and file creation. In newer settings it is called Create and edit files.
   * Paste your notes with the instructions Tally wrote for you and ask for a CSV file.
   * Download the file from the conversation.
   * Open your spreadsheet in Google Sheets.
   * At the top, click File, then Import.
   * Choose the file, pick Insert new sheets or Append rows to current sheet, then click Import.

**What Tally would write for her**

* Event planning instructions: Tells the assistant to turn your spoken notes into a checklist, a supplier to do list and a short team announcement.
* Event checklist template: Holds the usual parts of your events, such as food, drinks, setup and cleanup, so nothing is forgotten.
* Announcement style notes: Describes how you like event messages to your colleagues to sound.
* Table layout: Names the columns for your Excel event table so notes arrive ready to paste.

**Where she checks.** You compare the plan and the table with your own notes, and check every name, number and date before you share or import anything.
